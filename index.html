Over the years, I’ve moved from backend engineering and software architecture into leading technical teams and organizations at companies including Voi, Grace Health, Northvolt, and Aris Machina. My work has taken me through urban mobility, women’s health, sustainable energy, advanced manufacturing, data, and AI.

What connects those experiences is a desire to make difficult things useful.

Turning technical complexity into dependable products, helping talented people do their best work, and building organizations that can grow without losing clarity or humanity.

How I work

I believe strong engineering leadership is built on trust, transparency, and shared purpose. I stay close enough to the technology to understand the real challenges, while giving people the context and autonomy they need to solve them well.

My role is not to have every answer. It is to ask good questions, create clarity, share knowledge, and help teams make thoughtful decisions, even when the environment is moving quickly and the path ahead is uncertain.

I care about reliable systems, pragmatic architecture, and high standards. But technology is only part of the equation. The best products emerge when engineering, product, design, and business work together around a problem that genuinely matters.

Beyond the job title

I’m naturally curious and tend to follow questions further than most, whether they concern artificial intelligence, science, business, leadership, or how technology can interact more meaningfully with the physical world.

Outside work, I’m a father, partner, endurance athlete, and enthusiastic traveller. Training has taught me many of the same lessons I value professionally: consistency matters more than intensity, progress compounds quietly, and ambitious goals become possible when approached with patience.

Ambitious about my standards rather than my status.

I try to keep learning, remain humble, and build a life in which meaningful work, relationships, health, and curiosity reinforce one another.

What I’m exploring now

I’m currently taking time to train, travel, research emerging developments in AI, and think carefully about the next chapter of my career.

I’m particularly interested in engineering leadership where software and AI meet consequential, real-world problems, whether in AI-native products, physical systems, advanced industry, health, sustainability, or sport.

The right opportunity is not simply a bigger title. It is a difficult and worthwhile problem, a thoughtful group of people, and the freedom to build something that deserves to exist.
